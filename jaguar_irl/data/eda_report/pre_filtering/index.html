7. Net Squared Displacement

What is Net Squared Displacement (NSD)?

Net Squared Displacement measures the straight-line distance squared from an animal's starting location to each subsequent location over time. The shape of the NSD(t) curve reveals the animal's gross movement pattern:

  • Sedentary: NSD stays near zero throughout — the animal barely moves from its origin.
  • Resident: NSD fluctuates but returns to low values repeatedly — the animal has a stable home range and revisits areas near its start.
  • Disperser: NSD increases monotonically or reaches a high plateau and stays there — the animal has permanently moved away from its origin.

Classification logic used here (heuristic): For each individual, we compare the median NSD over the last 10 fixes to the maximum NSD observed. If the maximum NSD is very small (< 1 km²), the animal is Sedentary. If the final NSD is > 50% of the maximum, the animal is a Disperser (it ended far from its start). Otherwise, it is a Resident (it returned toward its origin).

Reference: Bunnefeld et al. (2011); Börger & Fryxell (2012)
