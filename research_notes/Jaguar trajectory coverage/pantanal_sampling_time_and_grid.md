### Which Hofmann assumptions can actually be met?

[Hofmann et al. (2024), Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11081933/) used random missingness and a simplified unchanging movement kernel in simulations. **Density cannot establish either property for a real jaguar.** Random artificial deletion is independent of coordinates by construction, conditional on originally observed fixes and protected endpoints. Natural missingness remains unknown; clustered deletion is a stress test, not a claim to reproduce collar failures. The native hourly record is still an incomplete reference.

An unchanging kernel is a simulation simplification, not a requirement that jaguars never change behavior. [Taiamã evidence](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0246233) describes diel and monthly variation in inferred movement states, and [Pantanal evidence](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/icwdm_usdanwrc/880/) describes seasonal changes in space use. Before fitted iSSA interpretation, assess conditional movement distributions by duration, verified local time, season/hydrological phase and individual; model or stratify relevant changes. Preserve both incoming and outgoing durations for turns. Do not infer homogeneity from a nonsignificant small-sample test or require a behavioral classifier for this preprocessing phase.

Residency is not a universal prerequisite for endpoint-conditioned route reconstruction or local iSSA. It becomes relevant for a resident/home-range estimand; [Morato et al. (2016)](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0168176) demonstrates why long monitoring alone does not establish it. No selected animal is certified here as having random natural missingness or a time-invariant kernel. Source timestamps are timezone-naive; no local-hour inference is made until timezone provenance is resolved.

### Development findings and changes to test

The pilot has run **610 mask scenarios on five weeks from three development animals**, comparing seven policies. Validation/evaluation animals have no mask-based outcome scores. Masks remove 0%, 10%, 25% or 40% of available interior two-hour-reference fixes, with random and two-slot clustered patterns and 20 replicates at each nonzero severity. Boundary anchors remain observed. Natural reference gaps remain; a missing reference fix is never manufactured. The initial controlled phase is even hourly slots; phase sensitivity remains to run.

At 25% random deletion, the strict-week baseline admits no window; the 68-of-85 density requirement already makes acceptance impossible. A combined local-bout policy, keeping the five-point minimum and actual four-hour hard cuts but removing the endpoint budget and whole-week loss/density/span gates, retains evidence in every scenario, averaging **59.0 observations per window** with equal animal weights. It brackets only **9.9 deliberately deleted fixes** per scenario on average: retaining a bout does not recover locations inside a long gap. Clustered deletion retains bouts but brackets far fewer deleted fixes. At 10% random deletion, strict-week admission is 90%, versus 100% for local bouts. These are development diagnostics, not validated superiority or model training results.

Even without added deletion, **7 of 294 evaluated hourly fixes bracketed by same-cell two-hour endpoints were outside that endpoint cell** across the five reference weeks. Same-cell endpoints therefore do not establish absence of an excursion. A wider 4 h 10 min tolerance improves 10% deletion admission to 95% in this small development sample; its ecological acceptability is not established.

Compare common scorable cases and separately report expanded-only cases and abstentions. Repeated masks are not independent animals.

The next executable stage needs dated, georeferenced cover and water/inundation layers with explicit validity periods and training-fitted scaling. Compare observed-reference habitat exposure/transition recovery and changes in the actual MaxEnt feature sums, normalizing consistently with the training objective; keep residence exposure separate from route feature counts. The repository has feature-export sketches but no verified historical raster/manifest binding for these selected weeks. Its existing quarterly covariate sketch also does not match the approved seasonal boundaries. **No habitat-fidelity result, iSSA fit, MaxEnt fit or new production cutoff is claimed.** Choose thresholds on development/validation evidence, then assess the reserved animals once. 1. Predict jaguar routes and relate route choice to landscape characteristics and change. Begin with endpoint-conditioned interpolation; unrestricted future-route/destination prediction is a separate task.
2. Quantify movement information and predictive accuracy/precision lost through coarse observation schedules, missing fixes, temporal standardization, and spatial aggregation. Distinguish preprocessing losses from model error and uncertainty in the observed reference.

**Use quality-controlled, unthinned observations as the primary source for spatial route demonstrations.** Retain fixes less than 1.5 hours apart. Use a common 500-m grid and transition definitions; do not interpret either GPS fixes or grid transitions as independent biological decisions.

**Two-hour thinning is an experimental treatment and a basis for comparable descriptive metrics, not a mathematical requirement of MaxEnt IRL.** Keep three linked representations: source observations, a standardized comparison track, and model-specific grid representations. A derived track's eligibility failure must not automatically exclude its source observations or animal from candidate groups.

The initial deliverable is `R_fine` versus `R_2h`: the same verified, route-only plain-MaxEnt architecture, trained on matched unthinned and two-hour-derived grid demonstrations. One-week, high-coverage windows in comparable calendar periods and bounded spatial groups constrain the first experiment's runtime. Window duration and coverage diagnostics must remain generic for longer, less complete records. Preserve source evidence without requiring every kind of evidence to enter this first model.

**Residence-aware and uncertainty-aware variants are future work, not prerequisites for the initial experiment.** Do not create a required `cell_runs.csv`, expand stays into time steps, add a stay action, fit HMMs, or run EM in the initial path. ## 2. Evidence and interpretation

- **MaxEnt and time:** [Ziebart et al. (2008)](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~bziebart/publications/maximum-entropy-inverse-reinforcement-learning.pdf) models state/action trajectories, not a mandatory wall-clock GPS interval. Consistent transition semantics are essential; irregular timestamps and unknown intervening states are different problems. [Ziebart's causal-entropy formulation](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~bziebart/) distinguishes the agent's policy from environmental transition probabilities.
- **Hirakawa:** [Hirakawa et al. (2018), Data treatment and Model evaluation](https://doi.org/10.1002/ecs2.2447) reports nominal one-minute GPS, selects 106 trajectories without large missing portions, collapses consecutive same-cell observations, replaces elapsed time with grid-movement count, and interpolates small grid gaps. Approximately 15 minutes is an average per resulting move. Evaluation removes large portions of held-out trajectories after learning from comparatively well-observed routes. This does not demonstrate robustness to poorly observed *training* routes. Route resolution relative to the grid matters more here than exact timestamp regularity.
- **Movement summaries:** [Noonan et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40462-019-0177-1) explains sampling-scale effects on observed distance and speed. Dividing displacement by elapsed time does not recover missed excursions. [Signer et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.4823) supports comparable sampling intervals for step-selection analysis, not a universal IRL constraint. Thinning does not guarantee independence; see [Fieberg et al. (2010)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2894958/).
- **Alvarenga:** [Alvarenga et al. (2021), §2.2](https://www.fs.usda.gov/rm/pubs_journals/2021/rmrs_2021_alvarenga_g001.pdf) connects fixes separated by at most four hours; 55% were an hour apart or less. It does not document constant-interval resampling. Raster grain and environmental neighborhood scale are different quantities.
- **Schoen:** the inspected [preparation](https://github.com/jaymschoen/jaguar_ml_movement_analysis/blob/main/01-data_prep.R), [HMM](https://github.com/jaymschoen/jaguar_ml_movement_analysis/blob/main/02-hmm_building.R), and [thinning](https://github.com/jaymschoen/jaguar_ml_movement_analysis/blob/main/08-data_thin.R) code uses rounded native modes with ±4-hour tolerance for eligibility, separate animal HMMs without elapsed-time inputs, and later row thinning that leaves multiple nominal intervals. Its [resource-selection target](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biocon.2025.110978) differs from common-duration movement increments. This does not resolve general metric comparability; Appendix A's numerical sensitivity results were not independently audited. Do not equate its ±4-hour buffer with our absolute four-hour comparison-track limit.

The supplied sensitivity screenshot reports, for 2 h/500 m, 65% same-cell steps, 55% adjacent nonzero hops, 76% nonzero hops of at most two cells, a five-cell 95th percentile, and 43 animals with at least 30 moves. Its provenance/implementation has not been reproduced. Its minimum-two-hour/maximum-four-hour sampler differs from the preferred 1.5–2.5-hour sampler below. Neither 43 animals nor those percentages establish weekly eligibility or adequate route observability. Recompute them under the implemented rules.

**Superseded alternative, retained for research context; do not implement as the default:** the earlier source-based ranking proposal below is replaced by the locked standardized-fix selection/ranking baseline. Source coverage may still be reported diagnostically. The former proposal was to count occupied two-hour bins in each member's parent window, using bins anchored at its window start; the final right endpoint belongs to the last bin. This caps the reward for dense GPS bursts without deleting observations. Count QC source observations in fragments passing the declared source count/gap criteria (§4C), including fragments entirely within one cell; retain the all-QC bin count as a separate catalog diagnostic. A member with no qualifying source fragment contributes zero bins. Let the ranking counts be `b_i`, and rank by `sum(b_i) × J`, where:

\[
J=\frac{(\sum_i b_i)^2}{n\sum_i b_i^2},\qquad
CV=\frac{\operatorname{sd}_{population}(b_i)}{\operatorname{mean}(b_i)}.
\]

Here `n` includes every group member, including zero contributors; define the score as zero and CV as unavailable if all counts are zero. Within the existing unflagged/flagged/relaxed ordering, place groups with at least two qualifying source contributors first, then order by this score. Tie-break by more such members, smaller maximum source gap, smaller mean of their median within-fragment source intervals (hours; equal member weights), smaller map area, then stable group key. Calculate each `usable_median_interval_hours` before grid collapse, without joining fragments across cuts; distinguish it from the full-window median in §5. Missing cadence values sort last. Display rank must match position within its stated list. Use the same eligibility/coverage principle to select member windows. Export `score_basis=retained_source_coverage`, the all-source catalog score, components and configurable bin width. Report moving-fragment versus same-cell-fragment coverage and per-backend capability separately; a group may be data-eligible but not yet fully supported by the legacy trainer. Bin occupancy is a coverage summary, not proof of resolved movement or independence. Also show raw-fix, standardized-fix and grid-length distributions, their CV/Jain values and histograms, with denominators labeled. Do not rank each model arm independently. Directional planned-schedule deviation is diagnostic by default (§5); it must not silently become an eligibility penalty.

### B. Standardized comparison track

Generate actual observed fixes only; no temporal interpolation. Implement this scoped policy explicitly:

1. Start at the first QC fix in the window. Relative to each retained timestamp, select the observation closest to +2 h in the inclusive [1.5, 2.5] h interval; ties choose the earlier time, then stable event ID. If none exists, retain the earliest later fix at least 1.5 h away. Gaps above 4 h will become boundaries. Stop when no such fix remains. Finer intervening fixes remain in A.
2. Define `M` as the number selected before segmentation. Split every edge above 4 h. Additional cuts are allowed at (2.5, 4] h edges. A cut removes an edge, not its endpoints; do not duplicate endpoints across segments.
3. Across final retained segments, let `A` count unique endpoints of remaining (2.5, 4] h edges. Require `A/M <= 0.20` for the entire parent window, not separately per segment. A shared endpoint counts once.
4. Drop fragments shorter than the configured `min_comparison_segment_points=5`, counting selected observations before grid compression/interpolation. Select the feasible partition maximizing final retained points `F`, then minimizing retained segment count, then total cuts, with chronological cut indices breaking remaining ties. Require `M > 0` and `F > 0`. Record final `F/M`; its optional minimum is configured by `--min-final-retention` / `min_final_retention_fraction`, default `0` (ratio gate disabled). Setting `.8` restores the former 80% gate. Changing the minimum changes this representation's eligibility; recompute F and all candidate diagnostics rather than merely relabeling existing outputs.
5. Remove the separate 5% fragment-loss cap and any implicit `F/M >= 0.95` rule. Record fragment loss diagnostically. `F/M` is retention of selected observations, not retention of raw fixes or completeness of an expected schedule. No hard-coded 68/85 or 80%-of-expected-schedule gate applies.
6. If the screen fails, retain A, the attempted B output and detailed diagnostics; mark B ineligible for the declared standardized comparison. Do not remove the animal from source-based grouping. Main/relaxed report categories cannot turn a failed standardized track into a passing one.

Reuse cached assets by individual/window/configuration key, with images under the report's `images/` folder.

**Directional summary statistic.** For interval durations `d_j > 0` and valid planned interval `p > 0`, use the **median schedule deviation (%)**:

\[
S=100\left(\frac{\operatorname{median}_j(d_j)}{p}-1\right).
\]

Zero indicates alignment; negative values mean finer sampling than planned; positive values mean coarser sampling. For a two-hour plan, a one-hour median gives -50%, and a four-hour median gives +100%. These percentages describe interval duration, not percentage change in sampling frequency. Display a textual finer/coarser label, actual median hours and planned hours; store the median-to-plan ratio as well. Do not take the absolute value: useful finer sampling should not be conflated with long gaps. This is a descriptive statistic, not a validated ecological quality index. Also retain arithmetic mean, IQR, p90, maximum and the fraction of intervals above four hours. A median can miss a minority of extreme gaps, so S must not replace tail/coverage diagnostics. Use standard linearly interpolated sample quantiles consistently and record interval count; with one interval, statistics may be calculated but carry a small-sample annotation. Missing schedule metadata makes S and the ratio unavailable, not evidence of adherence or failure.

**Ranking decision.** Finer actual observations generally offer more opportunities to resolve route details, subject to GPS noise and spatial resolution. However, schedule-relative deviation does not order absolute route observability: three-hour observations are finer than a six-hour plan but coarser than hourly observations that match an hourly plan. Use S to interpret schedule agreement, not as a direct score bonus/penalty or filtering violation. Rank primarily by usable-source coverage/evenness, then the explicit tie-breaks in §3, including smaller actual median intervals with equal animal weights. This rewards finer cadence only after coverage/usability checks and avoids an arbitrary weighted composite. Export the group median and maximum of individual S values, the number with valid schedules, and the actual-cadence tie-break value. S has zero direct ranking weight by default. Any later schedule-relative penalty needs a separately named, configurable sensitivity experiment supported by predictive validation; do not choose it from test performance. Distinguish schedule disagreement from actual route-support failures in badges and prose.

### Spatial actions, horizons and short demonstrations

**Action mapping.** `compute_trajectory_likelihood` uses `trans_index[drow+1,dcol+1]`. For offsets in {-1,0,1}, the row-major action number is `3*(drow+1)+(dcol+1)`; the center index 4 is replaced by -1 because staying is disallowed. The map is `[[0,1,2],[3,-1,5],[6,7,8]]`: NW/N/NE, W/invalid/E, SW/S/SE for rows increasing southward. It is grid bookkeeping, not a fitted shearwater transition matrix. Reuse it for jaguars under the same eight-neighbor convention. At 500-m cells the represented edge length is 500 m orthogonally and about 707 m diagonally, not a species-specific speed. Preserve the convention in the first implementation. Centralize/test the offsets and action order so likelihood, policy, rasterizer and propagation agree; do not make actions dynamic per animal or cadence. Changes to cell adjacency, barriers, diagonal corner crossing, longer jumps or future stays require an explicit shared dynamics/mask specification. Landscape features/rewards and any scientifically justified movement constraints provide ecological differences; the action IDs do not.

**Clock and prediction horizon.** Plain R predicts a distribution over routes between supplied endpoints at a specified spatial move horizon L. `load_trajectory` sets `start`/`end` from the first/last array rows; current `test.py` loads the full trajectory, so its terminal k effectively supplies a reference-derived horizon. The implementation does not predict how many hours until arrival. The 610 value is allocated step capacity, not 610 hours or the time between GPS observations. The adapted test interface must expose start/goal, `horizon_moves`, `horizon_source` (oracle versus operational), reference period, feature-map period and model limits in case metadata and report labels. A start/goal/horizon interpolator is not an unrestricted next-week destination forecast.

For a rough descriptive scale, report `elapsed_minutes_per_exported_move = 60*fragment_duration_hours/L` for L>0, and animal-macro median/IQR separately by fine/two-hour arm, source cadence and interpolation burden. For example, a supported 24-hour fragment with 48 exported moves averages 30 minutes per move in this descriptive ratio. This includes observation-scale waiting, missed excursions and constructed edges; it is not a calibrated clock or a timestamp for an individual move. Preserve the source interval bracketing each constructed edge; several inferred edges may share one interval. L=0 has no such ratio. Hirakawa's approximately 15-minute average is dataset-specific, not a conversion for jaguars. Consecutive cell changes can be hours or days apart after repeated cells are collapsed even when source fixes are frequent. The initial seven-day windows cannot contain a five-month span, and a five-month source gap is cut by the guard; k alone would not reveal either fact. Do not stamp predicted steps at equal fractions of a week and claim those are inferred movement times. Operational horizon distributions must be calibrated on training/validation data; the first reproducibility benchmark may use a common, explicitly labeled oracle horizon, separate from operational evaluation.

**Reward information, not an arbitrary five-fix rule.** For the repaired endpoint/horizon-conditioned model, a sole feasible route has probability one and gradient zero. Apply the five-observation eligibility screen first. Among qualifying fragments, mark a one-adjacent-move case `no_route_choice` and skip its optimizer work; record L=0 as `unsupported_residence_evidence`. For example, five source fixes `A,A,A,A,B` meet the observation floor but compress to a unique one-move route. Sub-five-observation fragments remain cataloged as `short_source_fragment`, not promoted to training merely because they move. Do not manufacture a longer horizon to create apparent information. Record `route_choice_status=unique|multiple|not_checked`; L=1 gives a cheap analytic check. A general feasible-path counter capped at two may reuse existing reachability work if profiling justifies it; do not enumerate paths or add a costly grid computation solely to screen every report candidate. Multiple routes still need differing feature totals to identify rewards. For the default informative-route fit, omit proven unique cases from both the objective numerator and step normalizer and report the resulting coverage/animal attrition; never leave their empirical counts in while removing their expected counts. Apply the declared paired inclusion rule consistently across arms. Retained source coverage is not equivalent to optimizer-ready information. Unit tests must give zero gradient for a unique route and feature-dependent relative probabilities on a tiny grid with alternatives.

### Future residence-aware modeling: evidence, scope and computational budget

**Deferred, not an initial prerequisite.** Keep the first R implementation spatial and stay-free. Its inability to learn residence is an explicit limitation, not a data-cleaning rationale. Repeated-cell source observations and timestamps remain recoverable through the checkpoint/event links; there is no required cell-run CSV, run-level reporting, time-grid expansion or stay backend in the initial deliverable.

**Evidence and its limits.** [Hirakawa's Discussion](https://doi.org/10.1002/ecs2.2447) identifies lost within-cell timing as future work. [Bracis, Bildstein and Mueller (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1111/ecog.03618) demonstrates ecological uses of residence/revisitation and daily timing, but is not an IRL performance study. [Hanks, Hooten and Alldredge (2015), §§2–4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1211.1992) supplies a stronger computational precedent: a continuous-time, discrete-space movement model for two mountain lions represents cell residence and neighbor-transition rates, fits using a GLM formulation, and reports seconds/minutes rather than hours/days for its comparisons. This supports a tractable direction to investigate, not proven accuracy gains or runtime guarantees for jaguar IRL. [Hanks and Hughes](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07986) further describes weighted Poisson-regression estimation with imputed paths. Neither method is a drop-in MaxEnt trainer, and uncertain crossings still need treatment.

**Concrete staged follow-up:**

1. Derive optional `cell_runs.csv` only for selected experiments, using one chronological pass over original event-to-cell links. Store cell/run/event references, count, first/last times and gap/QC metadata; never merge across cuts. This storage step is linear in observations and is not itself the costly model calculation. Repeated presence is not proof of continuous confinement; actual exit/entry times are interval-censored and excursions can be missed.
2. First benchmark a compact residence/rate companion model conditional on the same spatial grid, separately from route IRL. For neighbor rates q(s,s'), a continuous-time Markov model uses total exit rate lambda(s)=sum q(s,s'), waiting time Exp(lambda(s)), and next-cell probability q(s,s')/lambda(s). A completed residence/exit contributes q(s,s_next)*exp(-lambda(s)*duration); a censored residence has a survival contribution. These terms require inferred/uncertain path and censoring assumptions, not merely counting native fixes. Compare against a constant-rate null using held-out endpoint/exit events at common elapsed lags and runtime/peak memory. Do not assume exponential waiting is ecologically adequate.
3. Only then evaluate integration with IRL, choosing a duration/semi-Markov formulation or a fixed-clock stay-capable model. For the latter, declare a common delta, actual/imputed timing, features and H including stays; update value backups, policy, forward propagation, likelihood, masks and feature expectations consistently. The current code excludes center actions in all these calculations. Preserve leave-and-return alternatives and matched empirical/expected units; adding dwell only to observed feature counts is not a valid likelihood modification.
4. Benchmark runtime against cells S, horizon H, actions A and fragment count. Sparse eight-neighbor rate calculations avoid a dense S-by-S kernel; expanding every interval into many clock ticks increases H and can be costly. A fixed-horizon dynamic-programming implementation should target O(S*A*H) work per pass, but the current root implementation has additional full-tensor operations and must be profiled. Checkpoint/cell-run storage does not guarantee the coupled inference is cheap. Use capped workers, cached grids/features, sparse operators and explicit memory/time budgets; validate on tiny grids and synthetic known dwell/gap cases before scaling to longer windows.
5. Require predictive evidence before adopting the extension: held-out same-cell/exit-event log or Brier scores and calibration, alongside route accuracy and cost under matched observations. Observation spacing, GPS jitter, behavior and unobserved excursions can confound residence. Preserve animal-level evaluation. No current claim of learning resting, time-of-day effects or true continuous dwell follows from storing repeated-cell data.

### Animal identity, multiple demonstrations and the plain-MaxEnt objective

**What the original trainer assumes.** A `.npy` file is one demonstration with its own start, goal and move-count horizon. It is not an individual-specific model: `Trainer` supplies the same reward vector to every per-trajectory `MaxEntIRL` computation. The array contains `[row,col,k]`, with no animal identity. The current trainer reads basenames, averages trajectory log-likelihoods and expected feature counts over files, and does not encode which files share an animal. Each trajectory log-likelihood itself sums over its moves; file count is therefore not the same as animal count, nor does splitting a fixed amount of movement automatically multiply its evidence by the number of pieces. Nevertheless, animals with more total demonstrated movement can dominate the pooled objective, and file-wise partitioning can leak individual-specific information across train/test. Fix the separate empirical-normalization bug before interpreting this as a correct likelihood optimization.

**Hirakawa's trip unit.** [Appendix S1, pp.1–2](../../Hirakawa%20et%20al.%20-%202018%20-%20Supplement%201.pdf) extracts round-trip candidates at crossings of a boundary around the colony, keeps trips longer than eight hours, applies gap/distance selection, then explicitly retains only the outward journey from colony departure to the farthest point for the final 106 trajectories. Thus the stated demonstration unit is an outward leg of a selected trip, not an animal's entire monitoring record or a concatenation of its trips. This outward-only preparation applies to the final analysis dataset, not merely the artificial test gaps. The appendix does not provide an individual-to-trip mapping or establish the number of unique birds represented in those 106 trajectories. Do not infer the ratio, or animal-disjoint splitting, from filenames or trajectory counts. The root trainer does not itself detect or split round trips.

**Required identity contract.** Load a companion `trajectory_manifest` in the trainer/evaluator. Each initial entry contains `trajectory_id, individual_id, parent_window_id, source_bout_id, segment_id, pair_id, group_key, variant, model_backend, step_semantics, array_path, feature_map_key, source_event_ids_or_reference, grid_transition_count, fragment_duration_hours, data_eligible, backend_support_status, route_choice_status, split`. Use `model_backend=plain_maxent_route`, `step_semantics=spatial_move`; nullable array paths allow catalog-only fragments. No `cell_runs_path` is required. Future time/residence backends add their own clock/run fields and validation. The jaguar ID is a grouping key, never inferred from a filename or added as a landscape feature. Multiple segment IDs may share the same animal/window. Validate one owner per trajectory, compatible arrays/maps and original event provenance. Preserve independent start/goal/horizon conditioning; do not concatenate across missing intervals to make one file per animal. Gap-defined jaguar fragments are observation units, not automatically ecological trips or independent behavioral bouts.

**Proposed default weighting.** Optimize an animal-macro, window-macro, per-model-step conditional log-likelihood. Let N be training animals with eligible informative-route contributions, W_i their included windows, S_iw the contributing segments in window w, H_iws their spatial move count L, and T_iw = sum_s H_iws > 0. Exclude L=0 and proven unique-route cases from this fit/normalizer, retaining their catalog statuses; report windows/animals with no contributions rather than replacing zero denominators by one. This does not remove them from source grouping. Future stay/duration backends need separately declared model-step/exposure normalization. With c_iws denoting that segment's map/start/goal/horizon conditioning, maximize:

\[
\mathcal L(\theta)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i\frac{1}{W_i}\sum_w
\frac{\sum_{s\in S_{iw}}\log P_\theta(\tau_{iws}\mid c_{iws})}{T_{iw}}.
\]

This defines the target as average predictive log score per represented model step for an animal/window, rather than maximum total fit to the most heavily represented animals. Each segment receives coefficient `1/(N * W_i * T_iw)`; do not additionally divide by segment count. Apply that coefficient to its log-likelihood, empirical features, expected features and gradient, using the same departure-state reward convention. Consequently, the weighted model-step mass of each window is `1/(N * W_i)` and of each animal is `1/N`. An animal with ten fragments is not treated as ten animals. Longer contributing segments within a window contribute proportionally more spatial moves in the initial R fit. More fragments can still alter statistical information and conditioning; normalization does not undo those changes. A continuous-duration likelihood needs its own declared normalization and units; this discrete-step formula is not automatically transferable to it.

Use a configurable named objective, e.g. `animal_window_mean_step_loglik`, and save all coefficients/normalizers in run metadata. Reconcile eligible animals/windows across fine/two-hour paired arms before computing N and W_i. Apply the same formula in both; T_iw may differ because grid path lengths differ, so numerical segment coefficients need not be identical. This is an explicit change from the original file-average objective, not a requirement of MaxEnt theory. Include an unnormalized animal/window summed-log-likelihood sensitivity option if assessing the effect of length normalization. Never compare the two variants' raw training losses as the performance result.

Implement aggregation in `Trainer.__init__`, worker outputs and `backward_forward_pass`, using keyed manifest records rather than unlabelled file totals. Workers may process segments independently under the common theta, but the parent must validate returned trajectory IDs and combine the intended weights exactly once. Remove conflicting `/n_data` and `/n_feature` normalizations for the new objective; retain a separately named, corrected legacy objective only for reproduction. A new individual-specific reward vector or a different Bellman recursion is not required merely to group repeated demonstrations.

**Splits and validation.** Generate train/validation/test assignments from original animal IDs before constructing model lists. Every segment, period, sampling variant and appearance in another group of an animal must retain that assignment. Assert disjoint animal sets at train/test entry points; do not rely on basename lists alone. For very small groups, use clearly labeled leave-one-animal-out evaluation; tune on other animals or freeze hyperparameters, never on that fold's held-out animal. An experiment forecasting later movement of an already-seen animal requires a separate time-blocked protocol and answers a different question.

Use non-overlapping windows per animal within a pooled fit by default. Detect duplicate trajectories and reused source edges across group/window aliases; do not silently count them again. Separate group models can reuse an animal's data under the fixed split, but their resulting predictions are correlated and cannot be pooled as independent cases. Checkpoint selection and reported performance should aggregate within animal before across animals; uncertainty resampling uses whole animals, retaining their windows/segments together.

**Interpretation and limitations.** Group-aware splitting and weighting address leakage and unequal contribution; they do not make fragments biologically independent or model individual-specific preferences. The baseline remains one shared reward model per declared fit. Its summed segment scores are a working/composite objective for repeated demonstrations, not proof of joint independence. A hierarchical model such as `theta_i = theta_population + b_i`, or a persistent latent behavioral state, would be a separate extension with additional identifiability/data requirements. Cutting a route also changes its endpoint/horizon conditioning and removes long-range context; the likelihood of the resulting segments is not generally equivalent to that of the intact route. Do not claim exact split-invariance or infer a biological destination from an artificial gap boundary.

**Trainer acceptance tests.** Check manifest ownership and animal-disjoint folds across variants/groups; rejection of duplicate/overlapping evidence within a fit; weighted transition mass per window/animal; consistency of weighted likelihood/empirical/expected counts; weighted finite-difference gradients on tiny grids; worker-order invariance and missing/duplicate-result detection; fixed paired cohort with variant-specific normalizers; and animal-macro validation metrics. Keep these requirements in the MaxEnt training/evaluation layer; the movement processor supplies selected windows and provenance, not the statistical unit of inference.

For R, operational horizon distributions must be fitted using training/validation information available at prediction, then shared between arms. Marginalize/sample a feasible horizon set; report truncation. An identical finer-reference horizon supplied to both arms is an explicitly labeled **oracle-horizon diagnostic**. Never give each arm its own hidden test length. Neither `k*2 hours` nor a route's point count supplies a physical clock.

The general-purpose `movement_processor.py` and legacy models outside `jaguar_irl/data` remain outside this selection refactor. Root trainer changes are the explicit downstream integration task. Preserve existing behavior with separate configuration, not accidental shared-helper changes.

## 7. Future uMaxCausalEntIRL research and implementation

### Paper scope and extension boundary

This entire section is deferred beyond the initial plain-MaxEnt experiment. Its specifications and acceptance checks apply when this future backend is explicitly activated; they do not block initial source checkpoints, spatial exports, training or reporting.

[Bogert, Gui and Doshi (2022), *IRL with Partial Observations using the Principle of Uncertain Maximum Entropy*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.06988), §3.1/Algorithm 1 and §5, uses observation probabilities and EM to estimate feature expectations under uncertain trajectories, then applies the principle to maximum causal entropy IRL. Unlike treating each received location as exact, this allows uncertain observations as well as missing data. Its experiment assumes specified transition/observation functions and discrete observation steps; it does not supply a jaguar movement clock or demonstrate identification of unknown between-fix move counts.

First reproduce the paper's discrete toy formulation as a correctness fixture. Implement the following **project-specific time/space extension** separately. Latent paths represent unobserved movement, not automatically hidden biological mechanisms such as hunger. Learned reward, movement rate and GPS error can compensate for one another; do not claim unique identification from sparse endpoints.

### Treatment 1: explicit spatial observation likelihood

Let `omega_i` be the measured coordinate at time `t_i`, and `s` a grid cell. Supply an emission likelihood `e_i(s)=p(omega_i|s,Sigma_i)`. A concrete default integrates a Gaussian GPS-error density over a uniform location within that cell:

\[
e_i(s)=\frac{1}{|C_s|}\int_{C_s}\mathcal N(\omega_i;x,\Sigma_i)\,dx.
\]

This is a density over recorded coordinates, not an arbitrary cell-score normalization. Use device accuracy/calibration when available; otherwise declare and vary plausible error scales instead of inventing precision. Retain uncertainty at both observed endpoints. The initial emission factorization assumes conditionally independent GPS errors; test sensitivity to correlated errors, especially in dense tracks. Distinguish unknown/unsampled observations (no likelihood information) from documented scheduled-fix failures; habitat-dependent missingness requires an explicit detection model. Do not fabricate missed-ping events from an unknown schedule. Hold observation parameters fixed during the initial reward-learning EM; jointly estimating them is a separate extension.

### Treatment 2: elapsed-time multi-step kernel with latent move count

Separate environment dynamics `T(s'|s,a)` from learned policy `pi_theta(a|s)`. For a stationary policy, the induced kernel is:

\[
P_\theta(s,s')=\sum_a\pi_\theta(a|s)T(s'|s,a),\qquad
K_\theta(\Delta)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q_\eta(n\mid\Delta)P_\theta^n.
\]

`P^n` describes **n model transitions**, not n hours. The distribution `q_eta` is an explicit movement-clock assumption. A tractable first prototype is a homogeneous Poisson opportunity clock of rate `lambda` per hour:

\[
q_\lambda(n\mid\Delta)=e^{-\lambda\Delta}(\lambda\Delta)^n/n!,\qquad
K_\theta(\Delta)=\exp[\lambda\Delta(P_\theta-I)].
\]

This supplies a valid continuous-time process and integrates over variable counts, including zero, without rounding irregular timestamps. Holding periods and revisits are permitted. If `P` contains self-transitions, opportunities are not physical moves: report `J=sum 1[s_{m+1} != s_m]` separately from N. For an eight-neighbor jump kernel with no self-transitions, N counts cell changes. Horizontal/vertical and diagonal distances remain 500 and approximately 707 m, respectively. This is a stochastic movement approximation; it does not impose a hard speed bound or prove biological plausibility.

The mathematics follows CTMC construction/uniformization; see [Whitt, §3.4 and matrix-exponential representation](https://www.columbia.edu/~ww2040/4106S11/CTMCchapter121906.pdf). This clock is our extension, not a result attributed to Bogert. Fit/calibrate its rate using training data only, keep it shared/frozen for comparisons, and test sensitivity. Observed grid changes are an incomplete estimate of latent transitions. Validate waiting times/displacements; if the homogeneous clock fails, use explicitly specified state-dependent rates or a semi-Markov duration model, not arbitrary gap-to-count conversion.

For a finite-horizon/time-varying policy, replace powers by ordered kernels `P_0 P_1 ... P_{n-1}` and include the relevant clock/horizon state. Never apply a single stationary matrix power to the root trainer's horizon-dependent policy. Likewise, an arbitrary independent count distribution need not define a consistent process across observation subdivisions; test the semigroup property or implement the necessary duration/age state.

### Treatment 3: probabilistic trajectory bridge and EM

At observation times, use scaled/log-domain forward–backward recursions:

\[
\alpha_{i+1}(s')\propto e_{i+1}(s')\sum_s\alpha_i(s)K_\theta(\Delta_i)_{ss'}.
\]

Backward messages provide future-observation evidence. For one interval, the posterior over count and boundary states is proportional to:

\[
\alpha_i(s)\,q_\eta(n\mid\Delta_i)(P_\theta^n)_{ss'}
\,e_{i+1}(s')\,\beta_{i+1}(s').
\]

Conditional on exact boundary states and N=n, an internal bridge marginal is:

\[
p(s_m=u\mid s_0=s,s_n=s',n)
=\frac{(P_\theta^m)_{su}(P_\theta^{n-m})_{us'}}{(P_\theta^n)_{ss'}}.
\]

Handle zero-support bridges explicitly. Use analogous state/action transition marginals to accumulate expected feature counts; marginalize endpoint uncertainty and N. Infer whole bouts with all their observations, rather than fitting independent bridges that disagree at a shared uncertain ping. Sample complete posterior routes/counts and, where needed, event times. A same-cell endpoint pair can support either no movement or a leave-and-return excursion; do not remove loops or declare the endpoint absorbing merely because it was observed there.

In the E step, retain the distribution over paths, not one Viterbi/straight-line completion. With transition, clock, initial-state and observation factors fixed independently of reward weights, the stationary prototype's M-step objective, up to constants, is:

\[
Q(\theta\mid\theta_{old})=\sum_w v_w\,
\mathbb E_{\theta_{old}}\!\left[\sum_{m=0}^{N_w-1}
\log\pi_\theta(a_m\mid s_m)\mid\omega_w,t_w\right],
\]

where `v_w` is the declared animal/window weight. Use a discounted soft-Bellman causal policy (discount per decision opportunity, fixed entropy scale) and differentiate its actual objective. Endpoint conditioning or parameter-dependent initial/termination laws require their normalization terms as well. Do not assume the root trainer's feature-count gradient remains valid after changing dynamics, discounting and the random horizon. Verify the discrete-paper reduction and gradient numerically. Record observed-data likelihood, convergence and multiple starts; approximate EM need not deliver a global optimum.

Use sparse operator applications/forward–backward messages, not dense all-pairs powers or exhaustive path enumeration. Bound count tails, observation-support truncation and memory. A small prior Poisson tail alone is insufficient for a rare endpoint-conditioned bridge: check posterior truncation/likelihood convergence as bounds expand. Save approximation error diagnostics. [Tataru and Hobolth (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-12-465) provides relevant methods for endpoint-conditioned expected jump counts and dwell times; its biological application is different.

Required configuration: action/dynamics specification, stationary versus finite-horizon policy, discount/entropy scale, clock family/rate/calibration split, emission model/error scale, detection assumptions, count-tail tolerance, computation limits, EM tolerance/initializations, random seeds and feature convention. Unknown clock/error calibration is a modeling dependency to resolve before production, not an invitation to equate two hours with one move.

## 8. Common evaluation and metrics

For the initial R comparison, use identical held-out animals, periods, maps, observed endpoints, hidden observations and spatial resolution across paired arms. U/residence-specific metrics below are conditional future requirements, not initial deliverables. Hide intermediate reference observations from both prediction and tuning. Apply a common operational horizon/clock protocol wherever possible; when comparing R and U, identify different structural assumptions and include causal controls. Score against held-out measurements, not predictions from the fine-trained model.

| Target | Required reporting |
|---|---|
| Information lost before training | Paired observed cell/excursion/habitat recovery; polyline-length and fixed-window geometry changes; inferred-transition fractions. Separate evidence from imputation. |
| Spatial prediction accuracy | Hidden-observation-to-route distances in metres: mean/median and upper quantiles, over equal-sized route ensembles; also a truth-independent representative route, e.g. ensemble medoid. Never select the best sample using test truth. |
| Geometry and recovery | Secondary symmetric path/discrete Fréchet distances; observed-cell precision/recall/F1 and habitat-transition recovery. Report observed-anchor and interpolated-reference versions separately; an unobserved cell is not proven unused. |
| Predictive precision/calibration | Ensemble spread, coverage–area curves, corridor area/width at validation-fixed thresholds. Specify point coverage versus whole-route coverage. A narrow, wrong corridor is not good precision. |
| Probabilistic score | For U, held-out observation log predictive density marginalized over paths, durations and observation error, conditional only on permitted evidence. For R, same-reference-path per-transition log score at a shared oracle horizon is a diagnostic. Do not compare these different likelihood objects numerically as equivalent. |
| Landscape association | Common-map/scenario habitat-use differences, uncertainty and feature scaling; historical maps match observation dates. Counterfactual map predictions are not established causal effects. |

`sum(D, axis=time)` is expected accumulated occupancy, not probability of ever visiting a cell. Obtain visitation probabilities from route samples or hitting probabilities. Do not score normalized occupancy as a trajectory probability. Proper scores require a specified event/distribution; see [Gneiting and Raftery (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437).

Avoid endpoint error when endpoints are supplied. Use time-aligned errors only for a model with a validated timing distribution; they are not a common score for purely spatial R. Standardized empirical movement metrics use B; grid-route geometry can be compared at a shared spatial scale but is not automatically fixed-time sinuosity.

Report paired differences by animal/window, animal-macro summaries and uncertainty resampled by animal. Deduplicate reused cases across overlapping groups. Include straight-line and shared-constraints uninformative-route baselines. Report excluded cases, zero support, failed sampling, calibration/truncation sensitivity and small-sample limitations. Performance on finely observed animals need not generalize to the whole biome.
